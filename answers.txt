1. Pick the heaviest edge instead of the lightest. Since Python's
   PriorityQueue is a min-heap, I would put each edge in with its weight
   negated so the biggest one comes out first.
 
2. An MST connects every vertex with the least total weight, has no cycles,
   and has V - 1 edges. A TSP solution is one closed tour that visits every
   vertex once and comes back to the start. TSP is NP-hard, an MST is not.

4. With a binary heap it is O(E log V). Without one it is O(V^2). The
   difference is finding the cheapest edge: a heap does it in O(log n), but
   scanning all the candidates every step costs O(n).
 
5. Every step it takes the cheapest edge that reaches a new vertex and never
   goes back on it. That is a greedy choice. It still gives the best answer
   because the cheapest edge across any cut is always part of some MST.
 
6. The Fast Fourier Transform is called Cooley-Tukey, but Gauss came up with
   it around 1805. It computes the Fourier transform in O(n log n) instead
   of O(n^2). Gauss never published it on its own. It only showed up after
   he died, in his collected works (1866), written in Latin, so nobody
   noticed. Cooley and Tukey published it in 1965.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Fourier_transform
